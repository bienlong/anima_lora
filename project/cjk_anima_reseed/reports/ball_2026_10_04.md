From the user (10-04, after `stick_rk_jt50_2026_10_04.md`): the stick-only
re-fits move retrain_kana's read; how is the ball (the rows less their mean,
"the spikes") trained? The user's plan: learn the ball on grid / lone, set

**Verdict.**
- **The ball is learned in the low band; flat grids alone learn as much of
  it as the scene mixes.** On the 81 hiragana rows, `grid_small r0` (cold,
  flat grids only, σ 0.3–0.7) has a ball at same-row cos 0.46 to
  retrain_kana's — `kana_mix` 0.46, `anchor` 0.44, `kana_up` 0.49. The same
  items at 0.75–0.93 alone (`b7593`) leave a ball at 0.10 to every arm and a
  stick at cos 0.47–0.53: nothing glyph-shaped. A lower edge at 0
  (`recap_h0`) shortens the stick (78) and weakens the ball (0.24).
- **On retrain_kana's stick the grid ball reads at retrain_kana's level.**
  `gs_rkstick` (grid_small r0's hiragana rows less their mean plus
  retrain_kana's hiragana mean, every other row retrain_kana's), hiragana
  keys: words official 16 vs 11 (9 / 4, p 0.27), ≤ 1 edit 28 vs 25, dup 15
  vs 17; singles official 14 vs 17 (3 / 6, p 0.51). Against the same ball on
  its own stick (`gs_self`): words official 13 / 2 (p 0.007), dup 0 / 15
  (p 6e-5). What the grid run lacked was the stick, not the ball.
- **The ball carries the grid's picture.** On the words `gs_rkstick` pays
  in EN-ref cos (en_cos −0.018, 10 / 26, p 0.006; outside the box −0.016,
  p 0.03); on p01 it writes the word large and centred on a flat pink canvas
  where retrain_kana writes a top banner over the bed. `gs_self` keeps the
  scene (singles en_cos +0.025, p 0.10) and repeats. Singles tie retrain_kana
  on EN-ref cos. `stick_scene_2026_10_04.md`'s "the look is in the spikes"
  now has its grid case: the flat-canvas look reads out once the stick lets
  the ball read.
- **The user's read of the sheets** (10-04, `ball_sheets`): `gs_rkstick`
  renders the glyphs best of the three, but fits the text into the scene
  less well than retrain_kana.
